“In PCl5, the five sp3d hybrid orbitals of phosphorus overlap with the singly occupied 3p orbitals of chlorine atoms to form five P–Cl sigma bonds. Three P–Cl bonds lie in one plane and make an angle of 120° with each other (equatorial bonds). The other two P–Cl bonds lie above and below the equatorial plane, making an angle of 90° (axial bonds). The axial bonds experience greater repulsive forces from the equatorial bond pairs, therefore the axial bonds are slightly longer and less stable than the equatorial bonds.”

Q. Which of the following statements is correct regarding the structure and bonding in phosphorus pentachloride (PCl5)?

(A) All five P–Cl bonds are of equal length and strength.
(B)
Axial bonds are longer and weaker than equatorial bonds due to greater repulsions. [CORRECT KEY ✓]
(C) Equatorial bonds suffer greater repulsions than axial bonds.
(D) PCl5 has a regular trigonal bipyramidal structure with zero dipole moment in gas phase.
lightbulb EXECUTIVE NCERT CANONICAL EXPLANATION REF: NCERT-CH4-P114

In trigonal bipyramidal geometry (sp3d hybridization of phosphorus), the 3 equatorial bonds lie at 120° to each other, while the 2 axial bonds make 90° angles with all 3 equatorial bonds. Because 90° repulsions are significantly stronger than 120° repulsions, the axial electron pairs experience more repulsion and are pushed further outward. Hence, axial bonds are longer (240 pm) than equatorial bonds (202 pm).
